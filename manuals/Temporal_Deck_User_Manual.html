A turntable for recorded time

A turntable for recorded time

Temporal Deck is a 20 HP stereo scratch deck, live buffer, delay, and sample player. Incoming audio enters a circular recording buffer; a separate playback head can move through that history. Moving the platter changes playback position and speed together, producing turntable-style pitch bends and scratches. Normal platter speed is based on 33⅓ RPM.

Live audio

The recording head keeps collecting incoming sound while you explore earlier material. BUFFER limits the available history. FREEZE stops new recording so the captured material can be worked repeatedly.

Loaded samples

Loading a file switches to sample playback. The platter works through the file, BUFFER sets the active playback window, and the arc provides seeking. LongPlay uses disk-backed streaming for loaded files.

First patch

First patch

  1. Patch an audio source to IN L. Leave IN R empty for the same signal on both channels, or connect a separate right channel for stereo.
  2. Connect OUT L and OUT R to your mixer. Start with FB fully down and WET fully up.
  3. Leave RATE centered at 1× and let the live buffer collect some sound. Turn BUFFER up to make more history accessible.
  4. Click and drag around the platter to scrub backward and forward. Hold the platter still to hold the playback position; release to resume motion.
  5. Press SLIP to enable a return after scratching. Press FREEZE when you want to preserve the live recording.
Start with sound in the buffer. An empty live buffer has no recorded history to scratch. WET at zero passes the dry input and hides the deck’s playback.

Panel tour

Panel tour

Click a numbered marker to reach its description. The reference begins beside the panel and continues below it, including all four outputs.

1

BUFFER

Top left

Limits accessible live history within the selected buffer range. In sample mode it sets the active window end as a fraction of the file; it does not select a different recording allocation.

2

Platter & arc

Center and upper rim

Left-drag the record to scratch; scroll over it for wheel scratching. Middle-click requests a quick slip return. Click or drag the upper arc to seek through live history or the loaded sample, clamped to the active BUFFER window.

3

SENS

Lower left, above IN L

Adjusts scratch sensitivity. Tune this to your mouse or trackpad before performing fine movements.

4

SLIP

Beside SENS

Cycles through Slow → Normal → Instant → Off. The three small indicators show the enabled return setting. Slip brings playback back toward its running reference after a scratch.

5

RATE

Above the RATE input

Sets base playback speed from 0.5× to 2×, with 1× at the center. A cable in RATE replaces this knob setting.

6

REV

Gold button beside REV input

Toggles reverse playback when external gated control is not taking ownership. Enabling reverse clears a button-latched freeze; in sample mode it also starts playback.

7

FREEZE

Gold button above FREEZE input

Latches freeze. Enabling it clears reverse and slip. Live recording stops; in sample mode normal transport pauses. You can still manipulate the captured material with the platter.

8

WET & FB

Two knobs at lower right

WET blends dry input and deck playback, from dry to fully wet. FB feeds playback back into the live recording, from none to full feedback. Begin with FB down and raise it gradually.

9

Cartridge

Tonearm / cartridge area

The cartridge selector cycles Clean, M44-7, C.MKII S, 680 HP, Q.Bert, and Lo-Fi. These provide alternative playback coloration.

10

TD.Scope button

Small button at the upper right edge

Spawns the companion TD.Scope expander. It provides a larger waveform and position view; the deck can be used without it.

11

GATE — scratch gate input

Bottom row, below REV

A gate of at least 1 V engages external scratching. Pair it with POS to control the platter from CV, or connect another deck’s S.GATE here. Choose Glide / inertia or Module sync in the Gate+Pos context menu.

12

POS — scratch position input

Bottom row, between GATE and FREEZE

Sets relative scratch displacement while GATE is active, at 1 V per second over −10 to +10 V. Positive voltage moves forward toward NOW; negative voltage moves backward into history. Travel is limited by the available window. Connect another deck’s S.POS here to receive its gesture.

13

OUT L — output

Top right cyan jack

Left audio output. Carries the blend of dry input and deck playback set by WET. Connect OUT L and OUT R to a stereo mixer or output module.

14

OUT R — output

Bottom right cyan jack

Right audio output, with the same WET blend as OUT L. When IN R is unpatched, the left input supplies both recording channels.

15

S.GATE — output

Top left cyan jack

Scratch activity output: 10 V while scratching and 0 V otherwise. Connect to another deck’s GATE input and pair with S.POS to transmit the gesture.

16

S.POS — output

Bottom left cyan jack

Relative scratch displacement output, scaled at 1 V per second and limited to −10 to +10 V. Positive voltage means forward motion toward NOW. Returns to 0 V when S.GATE is low. Connect to another deck’s POS input.

Scratch, slip, reverse, and freeze

Scratch, slip, reverse, and freeze

Perform on the record

Use circular hand movements for vinyl-style motion. A held touch can stop playback even though live audio continues to be recorded. Wheel scratching offers another gesture path. Sensitivity and scratch smoothing affect the feel.

Choose the return

Slip’s Slow, Normal, and Instant settings determine how playback rejoins its reference. Select None in the menu to disable it. Middle-clicking the platter requests a quick return without cycling the SLIP button.

Freeze the material

FREEZE preserves live history by stopping writes, including feedback writes. It is distinct from simply touching the platter. In sample mode it pauses free-running transport while keeping the material available for scratching.

Transport interactions

The panel buttons interact: enabling FREEZE clears reverse and slip; enabling REV clears button-latched freeze; enabling a slip return clears button-latched freeze. A patched gated control can still impose its own state.

Samples and LongPlay

Samples and LongPlay

  1. Right-click and choose Load sample… to open a WAV, FLAC, or MP3 file. Supported audio files can also be dropped onto the module.
  2. Click the sample loop icon to toggle looping. Enabling looping starts playback; without looping, playback stops at the active window boundary. REV also starts sample playback, in reverse. FREEZE pauses normal transport.
  3. Set BUFFER to choose the active window end. Seek using the arc, or scratch the platter. Arc seeking addresses the full file and clamps to that window.
  4. Choose Clear sample to return to live buffering.

Capture the live buffer

Convert live → sample turns the currently accessible live window into sample material. Save sample… exports a live-converted sample as WAV. This export option is shown for live conversions.

LongPlay files

Loaded files automatically use disk-backed LongPlay rather than a selectable live RAM recording range. Sample info shows the loaded name and duration, and reports truncation when applicable. Keep source files available when reopening a patch.

Patch recipes

Patch recipes

Live scratch insert

Patch a drum loop into IN L, set RATE to 1×, WET to full, and FB to zero. Let the buffer fill, enable Normal slip, then drag the platter in short backward and forward gestures.

Frozen texture

Feed a sustained sound into the deck, then press FREEZE. Move the platter slowly through the capture. Keep WET high; use SENS to make small movements comfortable.

Two decks, one gesture

Patch the first deck’s S.GATE to the second deck’s GATE and S.POS to POS. Select Module sync on the receiving deck. Use matching source material and window settings when comparing their motion.

Feedback history

Start with a short live window and low FB. Move playback behind NOW, then blend it with the input using WET. Raise FB for recirculating textures; FREEZE stops further recording into the loop.

Troubleshooting

Troubleshooting

Why is scratching silent?

Confirm that the live buffer has received audio or a sample is loaded. Check WET, output routing, sample transport, and the active BUFFER window. A stationary platter or frozen sample transport can produce silence until you move it.

Why does RATE ignore the knob?

A connected RATE cable replaces the knob. At 0 V the external rate is 1×. Unplug RATE to restore knob control.

Why does the deck stay frozen or reversed?

Check the FREEZE and REV inputs and their gated/pulsed menu settings. Gated control can impose a state independently of the button. Check for a held negative voltage on FREEZE as well.

Why can I not reach an earlier sound?

BUFFER limits access, and live history is overwritten as recording continues. Increase the live range before recording a longer passage, or freeze/convert the material you want to keep.

Why does sample playback stop early?

BUFFER may shorten the active window. Increase it and check the loop state. Inspect Sample info if the source was reported as truncated.

Why is CPU or memory use higher?

Long live ranges increase RAM use, especially at higher sample rates. Sinc scratch interpolation is explicitly CPU heavy; try Cubic when reducing load.
