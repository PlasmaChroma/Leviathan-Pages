Integral Flux

A dual-slope generator, variable curvature slew limiter, precision attenuverter suite, and analog logic mix matrix.

Two independent outer function cores with sub-milli-Hertz to audio-rate speed, two central DC attenuverters, dedicated phase gates, and normalized OR / SUM / INV bus synthesis.

Core concept

Integration, transformation, and logic.

Integral Flux is built on two primary outer channels (CH1 and CH4) and two precision utility channels (CH2 and CH3). Channels 1 and 4 can operate as free-running or triggered function generators, LFOs, audio-rate VCOs, or voltage slew limiters with continuously variable curvature from logarithmic through linear to exponential.

Outer CoresCH1 & CH4 (Rise/Fall/Both)
CurvatureLog → Lin → Exp & Shark Fin
Normaled OffsetsCH2 (+10V) & CH3 (+5V)
Bus PickingVar patch normal disconnect
Analog LogicSUM, OR, & INV outputs

Signal routing

CH1/CH4 core → two parallel paths: direct UNITY output, and attenuverter → +/-10V clamp → variable OUT.

CH2/CH3 input (or +10V/+5V reference) → attenuverter → +/-10V clamp → variable OUT.

Each variable channel also feeds the internal bus while its OUT is unpatched: SUM = clamped sum; INV = −SUM; OR = maximum of zero and the four bus voltages. EOR/EOC gates follow core stage or slew direction independently.

Controls and I/O legend.

Sets the falling-stage time. Fall CV is the jack below and toward the outside of each SINK knob.

Four small knobs in a row, CH1 → CH4

Control the four numbered outputs and their bus contributions. Center = zero, counter-clockwise = inverted, clockwise = positive. CH2/CH3 use +10V/+5V references when their inputs are unpatched.

Inputs & outputs · 8–18

Purple-ringed jacks are inputs; cyan-ringed jacks are outputs. Rise and fall CV use slope glyphs; Cycle CV sits beside LOOP. The unity outputs carry ∫ 1 and ∫ 4 labels. Click a numbered marker to reach its description.

8

IN 1 / IN 4 — signal inputs

Outermost top jacks

Feed the CH1/CH4 slew limiters when idle. During a triggered or cycling function, the input perturbs the function core.

9

TRIG — trigger inputs

Top jacks beside IN 1 / IN 4

Left TRIG drives CH1; right TRIG drives CH4. Rising edge at 1V, re-arm at 0V. Triggers during rise are ignored; accepted triggers during fall reset the core to 0V.

10

Cycle CV inputs

Jacks beside the gold LOOP buttons

A gate at or above 2.5V enables cycling. These are separate from TRIG. Cycling stays on if the LOOP button is latched, even with low Cycle CV.

11

Rise CV inputs

Rising-slope glyphs beside SURGE

CH1 on the left; CH4 on the right. Positive CV lengthens rise; negative CV shortens it.

12

BOTH — combined timing CV

Jacks between CURVE and SINK

Affects rise and fall together. Positive CV speeds up; negative CV slows down. The saturating response is not calibrated 1V/oct.

13

Fall CV inputs

Falling-slope glyphs below SINK

CH1 on the left; CH4 on the right. Positive CV lengthens fall; negative CV shortens it.

14

IN 2 / IN 3 — utility inputs

Center pair above the attenuverters

Feed the CH2/CH3 attenuverters. Unpatched: IN 2 supplies +10V and IN 3 supplies +5V. These channels do not slew.

15

1 / 2 / 3 / 4 — variable outputs

Four middle jacks in the upper output row

Each output follows its matching attenuverter and clamps to +/-10V. Patching one removes that channel from SUM, INV, and OR.

16

EoR / EoC — gate outputs

Outer jacks in the upper output row

Left EoR belongs to CH1: high during fall. Right EoC belongs to CH4: high during rise. Both are low at idle; in slew mode they indicate downward/upward motion respectively.

17

∫ 1 / ∫ 4 — unity outputs

Outer jacks in the bottom output row

Direct CH1/CH4 core outputs, bypassing attenuverters and their clamps. Patching these keeps the variable channels in the mix bus. The integral-symbol labels identify these unity outputs.

18

OR / SUM / INV — mix outputs

Three middle jacks in the bottom row, left to right

OR selects the highest positive bus voltage. SUM adds the unpatched variable channels and clamps to +/-10V. INV is the negative of SUM.

CV and patching

Voltage behavior & normaling rules.

Integral Flux follows the classic Make Noise Maths normalization conventions with modernized high-speed CV response.

Port Type Range / Voltage Operational Behavior
IN 1 / IN 4 Signal In +/-10 V audio / CV Input to the shaped slew limiter. When not cycling or running a triggered function, the output tracks this input with the rise and fall slew rates set by the dials.
TRIG 1 / TRIG 4 Trigger In Rising edge at 1 V; re-arm at 0 V Starts a rise phase; triggers during rise are ignored. A trigger during fall resets the core to 0V before rising. A trigger from idle slew starts from the current core voltage. Accepted triggers have a 0.5 ms re-arm interval.
CYCLE 1 / CYCLE 4 Gate In High threshold 2.5 V Engages cycling at 2.5V or above, in addition to the latched Cycle button. A low gate cannot override a latched-on button. Releasing the gate lets the current cycle finish.
RISE CV / FALL CV Modulation +/-10 V exponential Exponential time scaling. Positive voltage increases time (slower); negative voltage decreases time (faster).
BOTH CV Modulation Bipolar, saturating speed control Scales both stage times: positive CV speeds up, negative CV slows down. The response is saturating rather than calibrated 1V/oct. Rise/fall ratio is preserved until timing limits intervene.
IN 2 / IN 3 Signal In Normaled to DC Unpatched normaling: IN 2 is normaled to +10V DC; IN 3 is normaled to +5V DC. Patching a jack overrides the internal DC reference.
1 / 4 (Variable OUT) Variable Out +/-10 V (attenuverted) Scaled by the respective CH1/CH4 attenuverter knob. Patching this jack disconnects the channel from the SUM, OR, and INV mix bus.
∫ 1 / ∫ 4 (Unity) Direct Out Nominal 0 to 10.2 V function Direct core output, bypassing the attenuverter and its +/-10V clamp. Slew output can be bipolar. Patching Unity does not remove the channel from the mix bus.
2 / 3 (Variable OUT) Variable Out Unpatched: +/-10 V / +/-5 V Attenuverted channel output, clamped to +/-10V with an external input. Unpatched input references are +10V on CH2 and +5V on CH3. Patching an output removes that channel from the mix bus.
EOR (CH1) Gate Out 0 V / +10 V End of Rise: +10V during Fall, 0V during Rise and idle. In slew mode, high while moving downward.
EOC (CH4) Gate Out 0 V / +10 V End of Cycle: +10V during Rise, 0V during Fall and idle. In slew mode, high while moving upward; this is not a short completion pulse.
SUM Mix Bus +/-10 V (clamped) Algebraic sum of all unpatched variable channels: V1 + V2 + V3 + V4. LED reflects SUM polarity (green = positive, red = negative).
INV Mix Bus +/-10 V (clamped) Inverted sum output: -SUM. Ideal for complementary ducking envelopes and cross-fade pan laws.
OR Mix Bus 0 to +10 V Analog OR / Peak Selector: outputs max(0, V1, V2, V3, V4). Creates complex composite envelopes and waveshaped harmonics.
Bus Picking Contract: Integral Flux isolates variable channels once their dedicated OUT jack is plugged in. To include an attenuverted channel in the SUM or OR bus, leave its dedicated output jack unpatched.

Modes of operation

Function generator vs Slew limiter.

Each outer channel seamlessly morphs between transient envelope generator, periodic oscillator, and external signal slewer based on how you patch it.

1. Function Generator Mode (Cycling / Triggered)

When triggered via TRIG IN or when CYCLE is engaged:

  • The unipolar function core rises toward 10.2V, then falls to 0V. Actual peak depends on rate, shape, and any patched signal; variable outputs clamp to +/-10V.
  • Rise CV / Fall CV control the slope speed exponentially.
  • Retriggering: An incoming trigger pulse during the Rise phase is ignored (ensuring complete attack transients). A trigger during the Fall phase immediately aborts decay and restarts Rise from 0V.
  • External Signal Injection: Patching a signal into SIGNAL IN while cycling gently perturbs the core toward the external signal, allowing soft synchronization and sub-harmonic locking.

2. Slew Limiter Mode (Continuous Signal Patched)

When a cable is connected to SIGNAL IN, Cycle is disabled, and no triggered function is active:

  • The channel acts as a precision voltage slew limiter (portamento / lag processor).
  • Rise Knob: Limits how fast the output can increase in voltage (slew rate limit on rising signals).
  • Fall Knob: Limits how fast the output can decrease in voltage (slew rate limit on falling signals).
  • Asymmetric Slew: Use fast Rise and slower Fall to follow positive peaks. For audio amplitude following, rectify bipolar audio externally first.

Curvature engine

Continuous mathematical warping.

Unlike simple linear slew processors, Integral Flux features continuous curvature reshaping that retains natural transient punch and analog envelope feel.

Curvature Position Rise Characteristic Fall Characteristic Musical Application
Full CCW (Logarithmic) Fast initial burst, slow rounding at crest Slow initial drop, faster finish Rounded attacks and falls that accelerate toward zero.
33% (Linear in Mirror mode) Constant slope rate (ramp up) Constant slope rate (ramp down) Clean pitch glissandi, symmetrical LFO triangles, precise modulation sweeps.
Full CW (Exponential) Slow creeping start, explosive peak rise Fast initial drop, gentler finish Accelerating attacks and decays that slow toward zero.
Shape Mode Switch: Each outer channel has its own panel switch. The table above describes Mirror mode:
• Mirror (Maths): Uses the same voltage-dependent slope law for rise and fall; linear is at 33% knob travel.
• Shark Fin: Reverses the curvature sign on rise while retaining the fall law; linear moves to 50% knob travel.

Visual inspection

Real-time waveform preview & frequency readout.

Each outer channel is accompanied by a dedicated visualizer window rendered with sub-pixel vector precision.

Vector Contour & Phase Dot

The display previews one nominal cycle of the function corresponding to the current Rise, Fall, and Shape knob settings:

  • Contour Line: Previews the selected curvature (log, lin, or exp).
  • Animated Dot: Tracks active function cycles at low rates; hidden above about 2.4 Hz and while the function generator is idle.
  • Instant Response: Contour geometry is cached and rebuilt when relevant settings change; the display still requires rendering work.

Adaptive Frequency Readout

Below each waveform window, the calculated frequency estimate (1 / (rise time + fall time)) adapts its units. It is not a measurement of external input frequency:

  • Audio Rates: Readout in Hertz and kilo-Hertz (e.g. 120 Hz, 1.05 kHz).
  • Sub-Audio Rates: Readout in milli-Hertz (mHz) (1 mHz = 1000 seconds per cycle). The readout rounds to whole mHz, so very slow rates may display 0 mHz.

Engine settings

Context menu options.

Right-click the panel to configure anti-aliasing and performance optimizations tailored to your CPU and patch complexity.

Setting Default Description
Bandlimited EOR/EOC Disabled Applies minBLEP anti-aliasing to the sharp edges of EOR and EOC gate transitions, reducing aliasing when using the gates as audio signals.
Bandlimited CH1/CH4 Signal Outputs Enabled Applies bandlimiting to sharp envelope reset edges when retriggering. Ideal when using Integral Flux as an audio oscillator.
Timing Update Rate 1 (Per-sample) Allows control-rate decimation (/4, /8, /16, /32) of timing parameter calculations under heavy CV modulation to reduce CPU load. Interpolate Timing Updates (enabled by default) smooths timing changes between updates.
Preview Tracer Enabled Toggles the low-rate animated phase tracer. Select Mirror or Shark Fin using each channel's panel switch.

Patch recipes

Six starting patches.

Integral Flux excels at transforming simple modular signals into animated acoustic movement.

Recipe 1

Percussive Attack-Decay (AD) Envelope

Goal: Tight, punchy envelope for VCA bass and drum plucks.

1. Patch gate/trigger sequencer into TRIG 1.

2. Set Rise to ~10% (snappy attack) and Fall to ~35% (medium decay).

3. Use Mirror mode and turn Shape clockwise from its 33% linear point for a decay that falls quickly then slows.

4. Connect UNITY 1 to your VCA CV input.

Why: In Mirror mode, the clockwise slope law moves fastest at high voltage, giving a fast initial decay and a gentler finish.
Recipe 2

Sub-Harmonic Clock Divider

Goal: Divide an incoming master clock by /2, /3, or /4 without dedicated divider modules.

1. Turn CYCLE 1 off and patch master clock into TRIG 1.

2. Take output from EOR 1 into your sequencer clock input.

3. Keep Fall short and lengthen Rise across multiple input clock periods. Tune while monitoring EOR; ratios depend on timing.

Why: Triggers during rise are ignored. EOR rises when that longer rise ends; triggers during fall are accepted, so lengthening fall is not a reliable division method.
Recipe 3

Asymmetric Pitch Portamento (Glissando)

Goal: Separate glide-up and glide-down slew rates for keyboard leads.

1. Patch 1V/Oct pitch CV into INPUT 1.

2. Ensure CYCLE 1 is OFF.

3. Set Rise to ~25% and Fall to 0%.

4. Patch UNITY 1 into your oscillator's V/Oct input.

Why: Notes played moving up the keyboard will glide slowly into pitch, while notes descending follow the faster fall rate. Minimum time is finite, so some lag remains.
Recipe 4

Cross-Modulated FM Complex Oscillator

Goal: Dual-operator audio FM voice without dedicated VCOs.

1. Enable CYCLE on both CH1 and CH4.

2. Turn Rise and Fall on both channels to near-minimum (frequency depends on Shape; watch the readout).

3. Patch OUT 1 into CH4 BOTH CV, and OUT 4 into CH1 FALL CV.

4. Listen to UNITY 1 or UNITY 4 through an audio output module. Patching OUT 1 and OUT 4 removes both from the internal mix bus.

Why: Cross-frequency modulation between the two independent slope cores produces rich bell-like sidebands and complex spectra.
Recipe 5

Analog Peak Envelope Follower

Goal: Extract dynamic amplitude contours from external acoustic audio.

1. Rectify acoustic audio with an external rectifier, then patch the unipolar signal into INPUT 4. Turn CYCLE 4 off and leave TRIG 4 unpatched.

2. Set Rise to 0% (fastest available rise).

3. Set Fall to ~30% (smooth decay between syllables or hits).

4. Patch UNITY 4 into a filter cutoff or VCA CV input.

Why: Fast rise follows positive peaks while slower fall smooths the rectified signal. The input is not internally full-wave rectified and minimum rise time is finite.
Recipe 6

OR Rectifier & Voltage Floor

Goal: Rectify a sine wave and set a positive voltage floor.

1. Patch an audio sine wave into INPUT 2.

2. Set CH2 gain to +75% (about 87.5% knob travel). Center CH1 and CH4 attenuverters and leave OUT 2/OUT 3 unpatched.

3. Monitor the OR output jack.

4. Adjust CH3 attenuverter knob (DC offset) to inject bias into the OR bus.

Why: OR selects max(0, V1, V2, V3, V4). A positive CH3 reference raises the output floor; it does not add an offset to CH2. This is rectification and peak selection, not wavefolding.

Troubleshooting

Common questions & answers.

Why isn't my channel appearing at the SUM or OR output?

Check if a cable is plugged into that channel's variable OUT jack (e.g., OUT 1, OUT 2, etc.). Patching a dedicated channel variable output jack removes that channel from the SUM, OR, and INV mix bus. For CH1/CH4, use UNITY to tap the core without removing the variable channel from the bus. CH2/CH3 have no separate unity outputs.

Why doesn't my trigger restart the envelope during attack?

Integral Flux uses this trigger rule: an incoming trigger pulse received during the Rise phase is ignored to protect the integrity of the attack transient. Incoming triggers will immediately restart from 0V during Fall; from idle slew, the rise begins at the current core voltage.

How do I achieve 1V/Oct tracking on the function generator?

BOTH CV uses a saturating hardware-inspired response, so it does not provide calibrated 1V/oct tracking. An attenuverter can adjust modulation depth but cannot make this response track accurately across the full range.

What do the dual LEDs on the mixer indicate?

The mixer LEDs represent the polarity and magnitude of the SUM bus: green indicates positive voltage (0 to +10V), while red indicates negative voltage (0 to -10V). Since INV is simply -SUM, the lights simultaneously show both buses.
