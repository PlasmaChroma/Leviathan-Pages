Proc

A compact 8 HP slope generator, shaped voltage slew limiter, envelope follower, and freeze controller.

Proc distills the core integration DSP of Integral Flux into a streamlined single-channel instrument with an external HALT freeze input, dedicated output amplitude scaling, and simultaneous complementary positive and inverted outputs.

Core concept

Direct synthesis, precision slew, and pause control.

Inspired by the classic single-channel lineage of Make Noise Function, Proc combines high-speed analog-modeled slope generation with modernized digital precision. Whether cycling as an audio VCO or low-frequency oscillator, slewing control voltages, following audio dynamics, or freezing in mid-flight via its HALT input, Proc provides complete mastery over voltage change over time.

Panel tour

Controls and I/O legend.

The 8 HP panel has five controls, a waveform preview, six inputs, and four outputs. Separate views below identify controls and jacks. Click a numbered marker to reach its description.

Controls & display · 1–6

1

LOOP button & LED

Gold button at top right

Latches continuous cycling on or off. The small light above the button shows cycle status. Proc has no separate Cycle CV input.

2

SURGE — rise time

Upper large knob on the right

Sets rise time. The adjacent rising-slope input (10) modulates it. Stage times depend on the knob, Curve, and CV; engine limits are 0.1 ms–1500 s per stage.

3

SINK — fall time

Lower large knob on the right

Sets fall time. Fall CV is the jack below and to the left (12). Equal rise/fall times at the linear Curve point produce a triangle.

4

CURVE — shape

Large knob on the left

Reshapes both stages and changes overall timing. Linear is at 33% travel. Proc has one curve law and no Mirror/Shark Fin switch.

5

Amplitude knob & readout

Small knob below INPUT

Sets the function amplitude target from 0 to 10V; default 8V. The readout shows the setting, not a measured output peak. AMP is bypassed during idle slew operation.

6

Waveform preview

Wide window below the controls

Shows nominal cycle geometry and a calculated frequency estimate. The low-rate dot tracks active functions and hides above about 2.4 Hz or while idle. This is not an input oscilloscope.

Inputs & outputs · 7–16

Purple-ringed jacks are inputs; cyan-ringed jacks are outputs. Rising and falling slope glyphs identify the two stage CV inputs. HALT pauses the core; TRIG starts a function.

7

INPUT — signal input

Top-left jack

Feeds the slew limiter when Cycle is off and no triggered function is active. During a function, a patched signal pulls the core voltage toward the input.

8

TRIG — trigger input

Top-center jack

Rising edge at 1V, re-arm at 0V. Triggers during rise or HALT are ignored. An accepted trigger during fall resets the core to 0V; from idle slew, rise starts at the current core voltage.

9

HALT — pause input

Left jack below the amplitude readout

At 2.5V or above, holds core voltage and gate states. AMP still scales a held function; existing bandlimiting tails can settle. Release resumes integration.

10

Rise CV input

Rising-slope glyph, left of SURGE

Positive CV lengthens rise; negative CV shortens it. This input is separate from TRIG.

11

BOTH — combined timing CV

Center jack between HALT and SINK

Changes rise and fall together. Positive CV speeds up; negative CV slows down. Its saturating response is not calibrated 1V/oct.

12

Fall CV input

Falling-slope glyph, below-left of SINK

Positive CV lengthens fall; negative CV shortens it.

13

EoR — gate output

Upper-left jack in the output block

Nominal 0/10V gate: high during fall, low during rise and idle. In slew operation, indicates downward motion. HALT preserves its state.

14

EoC — gate output

Upper-right jack in the output block

Nominal 0/10V gate: high during rise, low during fall and idle. In slew operation, indicates upward motion. HALT preserves its state.

15

OUT+ — main output

Bottom-left jack

Function output scaled by AMP, or the slewed input at unity gain while idle. Slewed bipolar input can make OUT+ negative; the plus sign identifies the non-inverted output.

16

OUT- — inverted output

Bottom-right jack

The exact negative of OUT+. In idle slew operation it inverts the slewed input; it can be positive when OUT+ is negative.

Voltage behavior & port reference.

Distinguishing characteristics

What makes Proc unique compared to Integral Flux.

While sharing the core variable-curvature integration engine of Integral Flux, Proc introduces specialized features for standalone utility and control.

1. HALT Integration Freeze

The HALT input pauses the core in any operating mode:

  • Applying a gate at or above 2.5V pauses integration and preserves the gate states.
  • The core voltage is held during rise, fall, or slew. In function mode, AMP remains active; hold it fixed for constant output voltage. Existing bandlimiting tails can settle after HALT engages.
  • Triggers are discarded while halted and automatic cycling pauses. On release, integration resumes from the held core state. A trigger held high through release needs a new low-to-high edge.

2. Dedicated AMP Attenuation Dial

Instead of requiring an external attenuator or central mixer channel:

  • The AMP knob sets a function amplitude target from 0 to 10V; actual peak depends on shape, rate, and signal injection.
  • Real-time numeric voltage readout right on the faceplate allows instant matching to standard 5V, 8V, or 10V Eurorack targets.
  • In idle slew operation, AMP is bypassed. An active triggered function takes priority over slew.

3. Simultaneous Complementary Outputs (+ / -)

Proc eliminates the need for an external inverter:

  • OUT+ yields positive modulation (0 to +AMP V).
  • OUT- generates exact negative mirror modulation (0 to -AMP V).
  • OUT+ can open a filter while OUT- reduces a positively biased VCA that accepts additive bipolar CV.

4. Dual Gate Directional Logic

Unlike Integral Flux where EOR belongs to CH1 and EOC belongs to CH4:

  • Proc features both EOR and EOC simultaneously on its single channel.
  • In Slew mode, EOC fires whenever the signal is slewing upward (delta > 0), and EOR fires whenever the signal is slewing downward (delta < 0), acting as a slope direction detector.

Operating modes

Envelope generator, oscillator, or slew limiter.

Proc adapts dynamically to your patch cable connections.

Envelope & Oscillator Mode

Engaged when LOOP is on or when pulses are sent to TRIG:

  • Synthesizes a clean unipolar function ramping toward the AMP target and falling to 0V; actual peak depends on rate, shape, and signal injection.
  • Cycle ON: Self-retriggers at the end of fall. Each stage can reach 1500 seconds (3000 seconds for a full cycle). Self-cycling is capped at 1 kHz; rate depends on Curve and CV as well as Surge/Sink.
  • Trigger Retrigger Rule: Triggers during Surge are ignored. Triggers during Sink reset immediately to 0V and rise.
  • External Perturbation: Patching audio into INPUT while cycling pulls the core voltage toward the input, altering the waveform. Synchronization depends on the input and timing settings.

Slew Limiter Mode

Engaged when a cable is patched into INPUT, Cycle is off, and no triggered function is active:

  • Limits how fast incoming voltages can change (voltage lag).
  • Surge: Limits upward voltage rate of change (glide up).
  • Sink: Limits downward voltage rate of change (glide down).
  • Envelope Follower: Use externally rectified audio, fast Surge, and slower Sink to follow positive peaks. Direct bipolar audio is not internally full-wave rectified.

Curvature engine

Logarithmic, linear, and exponential shapes.

The CURVE knob reshapes both Surge and Sink segments with mathematical phase normalization.

Curvature Setting Rise Response Fall Response Musical Applications
0% to 32% (Logarithmic) Rapid initial rise, rounded peak Slow initial drop, faster finish Rounded attacks and falls that accelerate toward zero.
33% (True Linear) Strict constant ramp rate Strict constant ramp rate Uncolored pitch glissandi, symmetric triangle LFOs, linear CV ramps.
34% to 100% (Exponential) Creeping start, steep explosive peak Fast initial drop, gentler finish Accelerating attacks and decays that slow toward zero.

Visual inspection

Waveform preview & frequency tracking.

Proc features a waveform preview window displaying real-time cycle geometry and timing.

Vector Contour & Phase Dot

The window renders the nominal cycle shape determined by Surge, Sink, and Curve:

  • Contour Curve: Updates interactively as knobs are turned using cached contour geometry.
  • Animated Dot: Live phase cursor shows active function cycles at low rates; hidden above about 2.4 Hz and while the function generator is idle.
  • Phosphor Persistence: Experimental CRT-style persistence controls are available only with Dragon King debug enabled.

Dynamic Frequency Readout

Below the visualizer, the calculated frequency estimate (1 / (rise time + fall time)) adapts its units. It is not a measurement of external input frequency:

  • mHz (Milli-Hertz): For cycles longer than 1 second (down to ~0.33 mHz nominal, or 50 minutes per cycle). The readout rounds to whole mHz, so very slow rates may display 0 mHz.
  • Hz & kHz: For audio-rate oscillation up to 1.0 kHz.

Patch recipes

Six starting patches.

Explore the sonic versatility of Proc with these creative starting configurations.

Recipe 1

Pause & Resume an LFO

Goal: Pause the internal LFO to create held sections in a pitch modulation signal.

1. Enable LOOP with Surge and Sink at ~1 Hz (slow LFO).

2. Patch a 16th-note gate sequence into HALT.

3. Patch OUT+ into a synth voice pitch input.

Why: With HALT low, the internal LFO moves; with HALT high, its core stops. This produces moving sections and holds, not a quantized arpeggio or instantaneous sampling of an external LFO.
Recipe 2

Complementary Sidechain Ducking

Goal: Duck a VCA that supports additive bipolar level CV.

1. Turn LOOP off, leave HALT low, and patch the kick trigger into TRIG.

2. Set Surge to minimum, Sink to ~25%, and Curve clockwise from the 33% linear point for a fast initial fall and gentler finish.

3. Set AMP to 5.0 V.

4. Open the VCA with its manual level or a positive bias, then add OUT- to its bipolar level CV input. Adjust CV depth to taste.

Why: The negative envelope reduces an already-open VCA level and returns toward 0V. A VCA that clamps negative CV and has no positive bias will remain closed.
Recipe 3

Slope Direction Detector

Goal: Detect whether a complex modulation source is moving up or down.

1. Turn LOOP off, leave TRIG unpatched and HALT low, then patch an LFO or sequencer into INPUT.

2. Set Surge and Sink to ~10% (slight smoothing).

3. Monitor EOC (high while voltage is rising) and EOR (high while voltage is falling).

Why: In Slew mode, Proc's gates reflect the real-time slope sign, driving distinct percussive events on ascending vs descending motion.
Recipe 4

Gate-Controlled Audio Freeze

Goal: Pause and resume an audio-rate function with a gate.

1. Turn Surge and Sink to near-minimum (watch the frequency estimate; Curve changes the rate).

2. Enable LOOP.

3. Patch a 0/10V rhythmic gate into HALT. High pauses the function; low lets it run. For run-on-high behavior, use a gate inverter that outputs a positive gate when its input is low.

4. Listen to OUT+.

Why: HALT holds the current voltage rather than muting the output. Monitor through an audio output module; gate transitions can click, and this patch is not FM or a phase-reset burst generator.
Recipe 5

Sub-Harmonic Pulse Divider

Goal: Divide an incoming master clock by /2, /3, or /4.

1. Turn LOOP off and patch a master clock into TRIG.

2. Patch EOR into your divided clock destination.

3. Keep Sink short and lengthen Surge across multiple clock periods. Tune while monitoring EOR; division ratio depends on timing.

Why: Triggers during Surge are ignored. EOR rises at the end of Surge; triggers during Sink are accepted and reset the function, so long Sink does not reliably divide the clock.
Recipe 6

5V Target VCF Envelope

Goal: Set a 5V function amplitude target for filter modulation.

1. Turn LOOP off, leave HALT low, and patch a gate into TRIG.

2. Turn AMP until the readout shows exactly 5.0 V.

3. Shape Surge and Sink to taste.

4. Patch OUT+ into your VCF Cutoff CV.

Why: AMP scales the function core; actual peaks depend on rate and shape, and bandlimiting can add transient deviations. Adjust the receiving filter's CV depth to taste.

Troubleshooting

Common questions & answers.

Why has Proc frozen and stopped responding to triggers?

Check the HALT input jack. If a cable is connected and the voltage is at least 2.5V, Proc's core is intentionally held in pause mode. Disconnecting or pulling HALT low resumes normal integration immediately.

Why doesn't the AMP knob affect my input signal in Slew mode?

By design, the AMP knob sets the amplitude target of internally generated function envelopes (0 to +AMP V). When Cycle is off and no triggered function is active, the patched signal is slewed with unity gain. During an active triggered or cycling function, AMP still applies and the input perturbs the core.

How do EOR and EOC differ from Integral Flux?

On Integral Flux, CH1 provides EOR and CH4 provides EOC. On Proc, both EOR and EOC are available simultaneously on the single channel! Furthermore, in Slew mode, EOC signals rising voltage and EOR signals falling voltage.

Where is true linear response on the CURVE knob?

Proc's Curve knob has true linear response at 33% knob travel. Values below 33% warp into logarithmic curves; values above 33% warp into exponential curves.
